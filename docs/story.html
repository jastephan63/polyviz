A Swiss canton in charts

LUSTAT Statistik Luzern, the statistical office of Canton Lucerne, opens its data one canton at a time — so this is not Switzerland in charts but one Swiss canton in charts, Lucerne as the lens. The portrait follows LUSTAT's own themes — Bevölkerung, Wirtschaft, Raum — through the portal's open datasets, and reaches for the package's bundled national data where the whole country sharpens the point. Every chart is live — hover, and take any of them along via the download button in the top-right corner — and every number in the text is computed from the data when the page is built. The canton counted 442,119 people at the end of 2025; here is where they live, work, and are headed.

Bevölkerung — the people

How many people the canton holds, how old they are, and how its capital measures up against the other Swiss cities.

Three roads out of the present

The canton counts 442,119 permanent residents at the end of 2025. LUSTAT's reference scenario carries that to 581,571 by 2055 — 31.5% more people — and the weaker and stronger variants bracket the future between 506,711 and 656,241. Even the cautious path adds 64,592 residents; on the reference path the canton passes the half-million mark around 2038.

The ageing wave

Growth is not spread evenly across the ages. In 2025, 19.3% of residents are 65 or older; by 2055 it is 23.8%. The 80-plus group grows fastest of all, from 24,470 to 49,893 people — 2.0 times as many — which is the dark band thickening at the top of the chart. The brightest diagonal is the canton's largest cohort, the roughly 35-year-olds of 2025 (born around 1990), climbing one row every five years.

The capital on the national ladder

Zoom out to the whole country and the cantonal capital holds a remarkably steady place: rank 8 among Switzerland's statistical cities in 1930, rank 7 in 2024. The path between was anything but steady — the city grew to 83,374 residents by 1970, lent 7,949 of them to its suburbs by 2000, and only passed its 1970 size again in 2024, at 86,234 residents. That dip-and-recover arc is the story of most large Swiss cities; Lucerne just drew it more sharply than its rank ever showed.

Wirtschaft — the economy

What the canton's people do for a living, what its municipalities spend, and how the tax base is shared out across the map.

What the capital does for a living

LUSTAT's open catalogue has no jobs file, so the federal enterprise statistics stand in for the capital. They show a service town with a twist: 21.6% of the city of Luzern's employees work in health and social work, 2.2 times the share of the next sector, trade & repair (9.7%). Just 30 of the 180 Swiss statistical cities lean harder on health — the cantonal hospital, clinics, and care homes are the capital's quiet economic anchor.

Where the municipal franc goes

Add up the operating accounts of every municipality and the canton's communal level spent CHF 3,200 million in 2025 — 31% more than in 2019, with internal charges between departments netted out. Education is the biggest item, 35% of the total, and grew 35% over the period; the fastest-growing major item is finances & taxes, up 83% in 6 years.

The fiscal map

The same economy looks very different across the canton's map. In the 2027 equalisation year, Meggen tops the resource index at 285.6 — 2.9 times the cantonal average of 100 — while Romoos sits at 37.2. 65 of the 78 municipalities fall below the average, and the resource equalisation moves CHF 156 million to 54 of them: the lakeside ring around the capital underwrites the rural west.

Raum — the territory

Where the coming growth will land, which places carry it, and what the ground it lands on looks like.

Where the growth lands

The reference scenario adds 139,452 residents to the canton — 31.5% — but drops them very unevenly on the map. Emmen grows fastest, by 75.7%, with Kriens (73.6%) and Buchrain (67.7%) behind it; at the other end Escholzmatt-Marbach is projected to shrink by 15.1%. Only 12 of the 79 municipalities out-grow the cantonal average, and 14 are projected to shrink — the coming growth is concentrated, not sprinkled.

Who carries the growth

Sorting the same 79 futures by today's size shows who carries the growth. The median municipality under 2,000 residents grows 6.6%, the mid-sized ones 17.5%, and the 7 municipalities over 10,000 grow 50.4% at the median — the canton's future is being built where people already are. The capital itself (87,384 residents in 2025) sits at 27.4%, below the canton-wide 31.5%.

The ground it lands on

LUSTAT's open catalogue carries no land-use file yet, so the federal Arealstatistik describes the ground all that growth lands on. Of the city of Luzern's 2,908 hectares, 49% is settlement — buildings alone cover 751 hectares — and yet farmland and forest still hold 1,416 hectares inside the city limits. Across all 180 Swiss statistical cities together, settlement covers 23% of the ground: the capital is dense even by urban standards, which is exactly why the beeswarm above shows its growth flowing to the municipalities around it.
